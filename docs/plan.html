Beacon Mesh

An open-source, peer-to-peer storm dashboard for Gainesville. Neighborhood sensor nodes share local readings, and when the internet goes down the dashboard keeps working, including a cached evacuation plan with shelters and a route out.

“Beacon Mesh” is a working name. Change it freely.

People in the Gainesville and Alachua County area who need to know when to leave, where to go, and how, even with no connection.

What the judges see

Hero demo, step by step

One storyline, told in order. Everything we build should serve one of these steps.

  1. Normal dayDashboard shows live readings from our real nodes, plus the simulated rain, water and wind inputs.
  2. Pressure starts fallingBarometric trend across nodes triggers a “storm approaching” signal on the map.
  3. Official alert arrivesNWS/NHC alert is pulled in and shown next to community readings, clearly labeled as official.
  4. Flood risk risesSoil moisture plus simulated rainfall and water level push the flood-risk layer up.
  5. Internet goes offA visible toggle cuts the connection. Nodes keep sharing over the mesh and the dashboard keeps updating.
  6. Evacuation plan, from cacheThe resident’s zone, open shelters and route to the nearest one appear with zero internet.

How it fits together

Architecture, high level

Three kinds of participants. Sensor nodes produce readings. Relay nodes (phones, laptops, boards) pass data along and cache it. A gateway node with internet pulls official feeds and the cache pack, then shares them into the mesh.

Before we write code

Open questions

Research

Which mesh software?

Candidates to compare, not yet judged:

  • Meshtastic: ready-made LoRa mesh on cheap boards. Very small message size.
  • Reticulum: flexible networking over many radio types, more work to build on.
  • libp2p or Hypercore: IP-based peer-to-peer, good for phones and laptops on Wi-Fi, not long-range radio.
Scope

What does “evacuate” mean in Gainesville?

Gainesville is inland, so classic coastal surge zones do not apply directly. We need to check Alachua County’s actual evacuation guidance (flood-prone areas, mobile homes, low-lying creek areas) and whether the demo also covers coastal neighbors who shelter here.

Limits

Video does not fit on a mesh

Low-bandwidth radio mesh cannot carry video. Camera tiles can only work while a gateway has internet, so the offline demo should rely on small data: readings, alerts and the cache pack.

Trust

Bad or fake readings

Anyone can send data. Plan for signed readings, comparison against neighboring nodes and official gauges, and a clear official versus community label.

Offline routing

How do we route without internet?

Need an approach for storing road data and computing a route on the device, limited to the Gainesville area.

Hardware

Exact parts and wiring

List the sensors and boards we can actually get at the event, and decide how simulated inputs will be fed in.

Boundaries

Not in scope for now

  • Replacing NWS or NHC forecasts. We add local readings next to them.
  • Live traffic or contraflow information. It needs the internet.
  • Statewide coverage. We focus on Gainesville and Alachua County.
  • Hazards beyond flood, surge and hurricane approach, for example wildfire or tornadoes.
